Teacher copy · do not print for studentsAnswer Key

Tasks 1, 4, 8, 9 and 10 are open responses. Marking notes are given for those.

01  Copy out the definition

Definition: "History (noun): the study of past events, especially in human affairs; and the written or spoken record we build from the evidence those events left behind."

historia means "inquiry", or "finding things out by asking".

02  Fill the gaps

survived · test · missing · failure · puzzle · more

03  Match the eight words

1 Source = B  ·  2 Evidence = H  ·  3 Chronology = D  ·  4 Cause and effect = G

5 Continuity and change = E  ·  6 Perspective = F  ·  7 Significance = A  ·  8 Contestability = C

04  Go hunting through the evidence

Open response. Look for six genuinely different labels drawn from at least three families, and for the "what a historian can learn" column being written in the student's own words rather than copied straight from the pop-up. The strongest answers explain how the source would be used, not just what it is.

05  Name the move

1 Go hunting  ·  2 Interrogate it  ·  3 Cross-check everything  ·  4 Ask a real question

5 Read very closely  ·  6 Interrogate it  ·  7 Build it, share it, revise it

06  The five questions that crack any source

1 Who made this?  ·  2 When was it made?  ·  3 Why was it made?  ·  4 Who was it for?  ·  5 Who is missing?

07  Put the timeline back together

Gold found, 1851, order 5  ·  Mabo decision, 3 June 1992, order 10

First Peoples, 65,000+ years ago, order 1  ·  Federation, 1 January 1901, order 7

Cook charts the east coast, 1770, order 3  ·  The Referendum, 27 May 1967, order 9

First Fleet, 26 January 1788, order 4  ·  Gallipoli, 25 April 1915, order 8

Eureka Stockade, 3 December 1854, order 6  ·  Visitors from the north, 1606 and before, order 2

08, 09 and 10  Open responses

08 Accept any of the eleven quotes, copied accurately and correctly attributed. The reasoning matters more than the choice.

09 Look for: different people saw different parts of the event; they had different interests and beliefs; they wanted different things to be true. The strongest answers add the page's rule, that different perspectives do not make every claim equally true, and cite a real example such as 26 January.

10 Any of the eight is correct. Mark the justification: does the student give a reason, and does it rule out the others rather than just praising the one they picked?
